# How to choose an English pronunciation practice tool

Start with the task you need to complete, not a star rating or a “best AI” headline. A useful tool for reading a short sentence may not be suitable for an interview simulation or a long presentation.

This checklist is maintained by Pronunciation Coach. It is not an independent ranking or a comparison based on tests of competing products.

## Match the workflow to the task

| Your task | What to check before choosing |
| --- | --- |
| Check a short English sentence | Does it support recording, replay, and feedback you can act on? |
| Assess your own presentation script | Does it explicitly accept custom text and recordings of that length? |
| Prepare for a conversation | Does it support interaction, or only reading aloud? |
| Practise regularly | Can you save progress or maintain a simple practice log? |
| Understand instructions in your first language | Is the interface translated, and what language does it actually assess? |
| Work within a budget | What counts as one use, when do allowances reset, and do retries count? |

## Questions to ask of any tool

### 1. What can I submit?

Look for clear text, duration, format, and language limits. “English pronunciation practice” does not necessarily mean you can upload any audio file or paste any script.

**Pronunciation Coach's stated scope:** supplied English sentences, up to 20 words and 20 seconds per recording; not custom scripts.

### 2. Can I hear my own attempt?

Replay matters because it lets you distinguish a speaking issue from a recording issue. A number without usable audio or an explanation is difficult to learn from.

### 3. What happens after the score?

Look for a next step: a word to rehearse, a phrase to repeat, or a specific focus for the next session. If the result only says “improve pronunciation,” ask whether it helps you act.

### 4. What does “free” actually include?

Check sign-in requirements, daily allowances, shared service limits, and whether a reassessment counts as another use. Do not assume recording, submitting, and completing an assessment are billed identically.

**Pronunciation Coach's stated scope:** Google sign-in; 3 free completed assessments per UTC day, subject to shared service limits. Recording and replay are not completed assessments. See [pricing](https://aipronunciationcoach.com/pricing) for current rules.

### 5. Where does my recording go?

Read the service's privacy policy rather than inferring privacy from the word “browser.” An application can record locally and still upload audio when you request a score.

**Pronunciation Coach's stated scope:** assessment sends audio for external speech processing. Consult its [privacy policy](https://aipronunciationcoach.com/privacy) for retention and processing details.

### 6. What does the tool not do?

A trustworthy description should make limits clear. If you need formal examination scoring, unrestricted custom text, or a clinical service, verify that explicitly instead of relying on a broad product name.

## Make a small comparison with your actual task

For each candidate, note one supported task, one relevant limitation, the cost of your intended usage, and a source URL checked on that date. If you personally test it, record the exact input and conditions. Otherwise mark it “not tested.”

Do not turn an untested product into a five-star recommendation. Do not copy an unsupported speed, accuracy, or user-count claim into a comparison table.
